---
name: Focus-group evidence discipline
description: Why the qualitative analysis uses conservative group-level coverage and verified primary-source excerpts.
---

Treat each supplied focus-group record as one group, not its summary and transcript as separate observations. Count breadth across groups, never participant votes or mention frequency.

**Why:** these records contain heavy repetition and automated transcription errors. Reliable speaker identities, participant totals and meeting dates are unavailable. The initial automated extraction supplied incorrect line references and sometimes treated summaries as direct evidence.

**How to apply:** verify every excerpt against the original transcript lines, retain the original wording, and keep Arabic interpretation separate. Do not promote a summary, facilitator recap or analytical proposal into independent participant demand. Flag uncertain terms instead of silently repairing them.

Related discussion is not automatically an explicit training need: general data use does not establish demand for advanced analytics, and governance alone does not establish demand for a complete ESG programme. Missing evidence does not mean opposition. Proposed course titles and implementation windows are suggestions, not agreed offerings or commitments.

**Why:** broad themes can hide materially different requests, and apparent interest did not always produce enrolment even for free programmes.

**How to apply:** retain contextual and unobserved signals separately from explicit needs, preserve equal-coverage ties, and test commercial viability independently rather than converting coverage into a sales forecast.
