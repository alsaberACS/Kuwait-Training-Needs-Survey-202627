name: Mixed-methods interpretation
description: Boundaries for comparing survey demand, focus-group evidence and catalogue training levels.
---

The user wants the qualitative infographic to integrate quantitative findings across the six training domains and then training levels, while retaining the qualitative briefing.

**Why:** this is an expansion of the existing research chapter, not a replacement of the detailed qualitative report or a new independent dataset.

**How to apply:** read the two samples side by side. Never join participant identities, turn group coverage into respondent percentages, or imply that broad domain agreement validates every course.

Training levels mean the existing catalogue's Foundation, Practitioner and Leadership tiers, not job seniority or measured learner proficiency.

**Why:** the questionnaire does not directly measure proficiency, and primary catalogue links provide the existing programme-level classification. Those extracted classifications remain provisional.

**How to apply:** distinguish actual course-level demand from analyst-proposed level design. A domain/level without a classified course is a catalogue gap, not evidence of no need.

Alignment is deliberately descriptive: fewer than five evaluators stays exploratory; the 50/100 rating-index cut is the midpoint corresponding to future interest, not a significance or consensus threshold.

**Why:** independent voluntary samples and broad qualitative themes cannot establish a statistical agreement rate. Strong-looking indices can arise from very few people.

**How to apply:** disclose thresholds and denominators next to results, preserve missing versus measured zero, count only direct explicit qualitative links, and retain narrower excerpt selections when a family spans several domains.
